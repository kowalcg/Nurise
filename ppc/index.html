📐 TACOS — the number that tells us if ads are building the business

TACOS = ad spend ÷ total sales (ads + organic together). ACOS only judges the ad clicks; TACOS shows whether advertising is lifting the whole listing — ad orders improve organic rank, and organic orders are free. A 60% ACOS with a low TACOS (under ~15%) means organic is carrying the product and the ads are affordable fuel; a high TACOS means we depend on paid traffic.

To fill this in we need one number from Seller Central: 30-day Ordered Product Sales for the filter ASIN (Reports → Business Reports → Detail Page Sales and Traffic by Child Item). Send it over and TACOS appears here — or authorize API access to sales data once (5 minutes) and it updates automatically every week alongside the rest of this page.

🧭 Why This Works

The traffic is excellent — the price was wrong

Core saluspa searches convert at 21–25%, which is outstanding for Amazon. The campaign wasn't broken; it was overpaying (up-and-down bidding pushed CPC 47% above the set bid) and leaking (~10% of spend to unfilterable broad-match queries). Fix the price and the leaks, keep the traffic, then raise the budget while the season lasts. At the $1.30–1.40 bids, the same clicks land at roughly 30–35% ACOS instead of 60%.
